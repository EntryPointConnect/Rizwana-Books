Mina and the Bees

About the Book

Mina and the Bees is a literary novel about migration, memory, healing, identity, belonging, and hope.

Leaving behind everything she has ever known, Mina arrives in a new country carrying the invisible weight of displacement, loss, and silence. Withdrawn and uncertain, she finds herself drawn to an overgrown garden and a forgotten beehive, where the quiet rhythm of nature begins to offer what words cannot.

Through the gentle hum of bees, the healing power of poetry, and unexpected acts of kindness, Mina slowly discovers the courage to find her own voice.

Mina and the Bees explores the emotional journey of a young girl learning to rebuild her life after migration. Beekeeping becomes a powerful metaphor for community, cooperation, resilience, and renewal, while nature offers Mina a path toward healing and new beginnings.

The Story

Mina and the Bees is a moving story about a young girl and her family as they navigate the challenges of displacement and starting over in a new country. As Mina becomes connected to an abandoned garden and a forgotten beehive, she begins to discover that healing can come through nature, creativity, friendship, and small acts of courage.

The bees become more than insects in Mina's world. They represent cooperation, community, persistence, and the possibility of rebuilding after loss. Through poetry and beekeeping, Mina gradually learns that finding a new home does not mean forgetting where she came from.

The story celebrates the resilience of children and families who cross borders carrying dreams of safety and opportunity, reminding us that healing often begins in the smallest acts of care.

Themes

  • Migration and displacement
  • Healing from trauma and loss
  • Identity and belonging
  • Family and resilience
  • Nature and renewal
  • Friendship and community
  • Courage and finding one's voice
